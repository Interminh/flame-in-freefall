## Summary

This ground study reanalyzed SPICE images to understand why soot formation differs between normal-gravity and microgravity flames. More than 1,600 raw DSLR images were decoded and analyzed with color-ratio pyrometry to get soot temperature fields, then calibrated light measurements gave soot volume fractions. A soot model including radiation re-absorption and aggregate growth was run for Yale and ACME burner setups under normal and microgravity conditions. Comparisons guided tests of how sensitive the results are to model parameters. The hypothesis is that microgravity flames are longer, more diffuse, cooler and sootier. The record gives no numerical results. Interpretation: derived soot and temperature fields from SPICE, not new flame tests.
