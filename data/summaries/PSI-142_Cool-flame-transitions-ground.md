## Summary

This ground study used the FLEX flight data plus new simulations and laboratory experiments on ozone-sensitized counterflow flames to map when cool flames, hot flames and the warm flames between them can exist. Aims were a new diagram of cool-flame flammability limits shaped by radiation, diffusion and convection, links to hot-flame extinction limits, and new extinction, temperature and species data to test kinetic and radiation models. Simulations covered n-heptane/air and dimethyl ether flames, up to 20 atm. The record says warm flames have a double reaction zone and matter in the transition between cool and hot flames. Interpretation: chemistry background, not material fire safety.
