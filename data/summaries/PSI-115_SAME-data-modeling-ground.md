## Summary

This ground modeling study used numerical simulation to compare how smoke particles grow in microgravity and in Earth gravity. It simulated dibutyl-phthalate (DBP) particles from pyrolysis vapor released by a heated sample, in a geometry similar to the SAME experiments, using direct numerical simulation at two low cross-flow velocities. It examined how thermophoresis, particle settling and flow geometry shape particle size distributions, and how buoyancy changes the wake. The stated hypothesis is that without buoyancy, mixing and heat transfer fall and the smoke composition changes. The record gives no numerical results. Interpretation: smoke-growth modeling relevant to detector design, with no flame experiments.
