About Ignis_0G

Ignis_0G is our entry for the Flame in Freefall challenge. It summarizes NASA's microgravity combustion investigations from the PSI database and ranks them for fire safety, for technical readers who are short on time.

What is in it

Investigations
All 24 PSI investigations. Each opens to a summary, graphs drawn from its PSI data, figures from NASA papers, the raw tables and every publication PSI lists for it.
Simulation
Estimates whether a solid fuel keeps burning at 0 g, on the Moon, on Mars or on Earth, using the BASS-II tests. It is an estimate with a range, not a prediction.
Presentation
The slides for our NASA Space Apps 2026 entry, to read here or download as a PDF.
Ignis AI
Ranks the 24 investigations against your question and explains the top three, using only fields from our investigation data.

How to read the results

  • Measured. Tests and tables are shown as NASA recorded them in PSI. Where the record states no result, the page says so.
  • Estimated. The Simulation is a simplified model. Every test used to fit it flew in microgravity, so results at other gravity levels are extrapolations tied to published NASA numbers. It is not NASA-endorsed.
  • Interpretation. Notes marked as interpretation, Ignis AI scores and the help chat are our reading or AI output, not results from the investigations. They can be wrong, so check each against its PSI record.

Where the material comes from

Investigation data comes from NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI). Reports and figures come from NASA's technical reports server and from documents hosted on the PSI records. Photos come from the NASA Image and Video Library and show related hardware or sessions, not results. Our sources cites all of it.
